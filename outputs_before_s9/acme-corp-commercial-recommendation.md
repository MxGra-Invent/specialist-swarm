# Commercial Recommendation — Acme Corp Enterprise Data Platform

> **Bottom line:** Open at $704K ACV / $3.9M five-year, 20% off list. Walk to 25% only with multi-year + reference-customer commitments. Refuse 35%, MFN, Net 90, uncapped liability, and a hard Year 4–5 price lock — all are playbook red-lines or structurally fatal to margin.

## 2. Discount: Open, Walk-Away, and Justification

| Position | Discount | Resulting ACV |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | 20% off list | $704K/yr |
| **Walk-away** | 25% off list (strategic max, requires VP sign-off) | $660K/yr |
| **Hard floor** | 25% — do not cross | — |

**Justification for refusing 35%:** The playbook strategic maximum for a deal in the $500K–$1M band is 25%. For deals above $1M (which Year 1 hits inclusive of implementation), the strategic ceiling is 30% — but only with VP sign-off and a qualifying condition (multi-year + reference customer). Even at 30%, Acme's ACV ($616K) barely clears Globex — an account Acme should price above. At 35%, ACV drops to $572K, *below* Globex — pricing a larger, more complex customer worse than a smaller one, destroying our anchor logic and signaling to Procurement that our list prices are fictitious.

**Pied Piper lesson:** Pied Piper was lost despite matching the 35% floor. Price alone did not win that deal. Acme's evaluation weighting is 25% commercial terms and 20% TCO — the remaining 55% is functional fit, implementation risk, and financial stability. Lead with TCO narrative; hold the discount line.

**Opening strategy:** Counter Acme's 35% demand by reframing to total TCO across the 5-year horizon, not headline discount. Offer to credit POC/pilot fees against Year 1 (playbook allows this) as a goodwill concession that costs us nothing if they sign.

**Strategic designation unlock:** Multi-year commitment (3+ years — Acme wants this) qualifies. If they also sign a reference customer agreement (one lighthouse case study for IoT/manufacturing vertical), push for VP sign-off and move to 25%.

## 3. Payment Terms Counter

**RFP demand:** Net 90 annual in advance.
**Playbook:** Net 90+ is a hard no. Monthly billing is a hard no.

**Counter-offer (tiered):**
1. **Preferred:** Annual upfront, Net 30.
2. **Acceptable concession:** Annual upfront, Net 60 — Fortune 500 accommodation, no price change.
3. **Fallback if needed:** Quarterly billing, Net 30 — no price change, but adds invoicing overhead; flag to Finance before offering.

Do not accept Net 90. Frame it as a credit-risk and cash-flow constraint, not a policy limitation. Acme is creditworthy ($1.4B revenue) — Net 60 is a reasonable meet-in-the-middle.

---

## 4. Five-Year Price Lock — Counter-Structure

**RFP demand:** Full 5-year pricing fixed at signature, no escalators.

**What to accept:** Years 1–3 at fixed pricing. This is consistent with our 3-year base term structure.

**What to refuse:** Locking Year 4–5 renewal rates at contract signature. At signature we cannot price against 2029–2030 infrastructure, compliance, or currency costs.

**Hedge to build in:**
- Year 4–5 renewal rates indexed at **CPI + 2%, capped at 5% per year** (standard playbook escalator for 3-year deals).
- Alternatively, offer a **volume-band mechanism**: if Acme grows beyond 350 TB by Year 3, Year 4–5 rates are negotiated based on then-current volume pricing — protecting both parties.
- In contract language: "Renewal pricing will be mutually agreed not less than 180 days before the renewal term, subject to a maximum increase of 5% per annum."

Frame this as protecting Acme from vendor financial risk, not as a price-increase mechanism.

---

## 5. MFN Clause — Commercial View

**Position: Reject. Hard stop.**

The MFN demand (§3.4) is the single most commercially poisonous clause in the RFP. If Acme's pricing becomes the floor for every comparable deal we sign thereafter, it retroactively reprices our entire book of business at Acme's negotiated rate — compressing margin industry-wide and creating legal exposure on every future deal where we discount differently.

**Counter:** Offer an **annual pricing review right** — we will certify annually that Acme's discount tier remains consistent with comparable accounts in terms of volume and term. This gives Acme substantive assurance without binding MFN language. Legal will draft the specifics; commercially, this is the only form we can accept.

## 7. Concession Ladder — Ranked

**Give first (low cost, high perceived value):**
- Net 60 payment terms (instead of Net 30)
- POC/pilot fees credited to Year 1 ACV
- Custom MSA (we do this on all >$500K deals; costs us legal review time only)
- 30-day acceptance testing period
- Year-end volume true-up with 10% buffer above committed volume

**Give second (meaningful, requires VP approval):**
- Push discount from 20% to 25% — conditioned on (a) 3-year signed commitment and (b) reference customer agreement
- Quarterly billing at no surcharge (flag to Finance first)

**Last resort / walk-away line:**
- 30% discount — only if annual deal value is confirmed above $1M inclusive of services, VP Sales signs off, AND Acme commits to reference case study. This is the absolute floor.
- Do NOT accept 35%.

**Never give (hard stops):**
- MFN / most-favoured-nation pricing
- Uncapped data breach liability (counter: cap at 24 months of fees, with regulatory-fine carve-out up to $5M)
- Year 4–5 rates fixed at Year 1 levels
- Termination for convenience with full refund (counter: pro-rated credit only, no cash refund)
- IP assignment for platform configurations (counter: license grant to Acme's data and configurations; core platform IP stays with us)
- Unlimited audit rights without notice (counter: annual scheduled audit + one unscheduled per year with 48hr notice)
